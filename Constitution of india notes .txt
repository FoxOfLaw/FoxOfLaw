CONSTITUTION OF INDIA NOTES 
Part I: The Union & Its Territory (Articles 1–4)
	•	Article 1: Name and territory of the Union ("India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"). 
	•	Article 2: Admission or establishment of new States into the Union. 
	•	Article 3: Formation of new States and alteration of areas, boundaries, or names of existing States. 
	•	Article 4: Laws made under Articles 2 and 3 do not count as Constitutional Amendments under Article 368. 
Part II: Citizenship (Articles 5–11)
	•	Article 5: Citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution (domicile-based). 
	•	Article 6: Citizenship rights of persons who migrated from Pakistan to India. 
	•	Article 7: Rights of citizenship of certain migrants to Pakistan who returned to India. 
	•	Article 8: Rights of citizenship of persons of Indian origin residing outside India. 
	•	Article 9: Automatic termination of citizenship upon voluntarily acquiring citizenship of a foreign State. 
	•	Article 10: Continuance of citizenship rights subject to Parliamentary laws. 
	•	Article 11: Power of Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law (e.g., Citizenship Act, 1955). 
Part III: Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)
General & Right to Equality

	•	Article 12: Definition of the "State" for Fundamental Rights enforcement. 
	•	Article 13: Judicial review—laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights are void. 
	•	Article 14: Equality before law and equal protection of the laws. 
	•	Article 15: Prohibition of discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. 
	•	Article 16: Equality of opportunity in matters of public employment. 
	•	Article 17: Abolition of Untouchability and prohibition of its practice. 
	•	Article 18: Abolition of titles (except military and academic distinctions). 
Right to Freedom

	•	Article 19: Protection of six basic freedoms: speech & expression, assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession. 
	•	Article 20: Protection regarding conviction for offenses (protection against ex-post facto laws, double jeopardy, and self-incrimination). 
	•	Article 21: Protection of life and personal liberty. 
	•	Article 21A: Right to free and compulsory education for children aged 6 to 14. 
	•	Article 22: Protection against arrest and detention in certain cases. 
Right Against Exploitation

	•	Article 23: Prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labor (begar). 
	•	Article 24: Prohibition of employment of children in factories, mines, and hazardous work. 
Right to Freedom of Religion

	•	Article 25: Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice, and propagation of religion. 
	•	Article 26: Freedom to manage religious affairs. 
	•	Article 27: Freedom from payment of taxes for the promotion of any particular religion. 
	•	Article 28: Freedom from attending religious instruction or worship in state-aided educational institutions. 
Cultural, Educational & Constitutional Remedies

	•	Article 29: Protection of language, script, and culture of minorities. 
	•	Article 30: Right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions. 
	•	Article 32: Right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of rights (Writs: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari). 
	•	Article 33–35: Power of Parliament to modify rights for armed forces, martial law application, and legislation to give effect to Part III. 
Part IV: Directive Principles of State Policy (Articles 36–51)
	•	Article 36–37: Definition of State and application of non-justiciable Directive Principles. 
	•	Article 38: State to secure a social order for the promotion of the welfare of the people. 
	•	Article 39: Policy principles (equal pay for equal work, resource distribution, protection of youth). 
	•	Article 39A: Equal justice and free legal aid. 
	•	Article 40: Organization of Village Panchayats. 
	•	Article 41: Right to work, to education, and to public assistance in certain cases. 
	•	Article 42: Just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief. 
	•	Article 43 / 43A / 43B: Living wage for workers, workers' participation in management, and co-operative societies. 
	•	Article 44: Uniform Civil Code (UCC) for citizens. 
	•	Article 45: Early childhood care and education to children below 6 years. 
	•	Article 46: Promotion of educational and economic interests of SCs, STs, and weaker sections. 
	•	Article 47: Raising level of nutrition, standard of living, and prohibition of intoxicating drinks/drugs. 
	•	Article 48: Organization of agriculture and animal husbandry (prohibition of cow slaughter). 
	•	Article 48A: Protection and improvement of environment, forests, and wildlife. 
	•	Article 49: Protection of monuments, places, and objects of national importance. 
	•	Article 50: Separation of the judiciary from the executive. 
	•	Article 51: Promotion of international peace and security. 
Part IV-A: Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)
	•	Article 51A: Enlistment of 11 Fundamental Duties for Indian citizens (clause (a) to (k)). 
Part V: The Union Government (Articles 52–151)

The Executive (Articles 52–78)


	•	Article 52 & 53: President of India and executive power of the Union.  
	•	Article 54 & 55: Election and manner of election of the President.  
	•	Article 56 & 60: Term of office and Oath/affirmation by the President.  
	•	Article 61: Procedure for impeachment of the President. 
	•	Article 63–66: Vice-President of India, ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha, and election procedure. 
	•	Article 72: Power of President to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, or remissions of punishment. 
	•	Article 74 & 75: Council of Ministers with Prime Minister at head to aid and advise the President. 
	•	Article 76: Attorney-General for India. 
	•	Article 78: Duties of Prime Minister regarding furnishing information to the President. 
Parliament (Articles 79–122)

	•	Article 79: Constitution of Parliament (President, Rajya Sabha, Lok Sabha). 
	•	Article 80 & 81: Composition of Rajya Sabha (Council of States) and Lok Sabha (House of the People). 
	•	Article 83 & 85: Duration of Houses, sessions of Parliament, prorogation, and dissolution.  
	•	Article 108: Joint sitting of both Houses in certain cases.  
	•	Article 109 & 110: Special procedure for Money Bills and definition of "Money Bill".  
	•	Article 112: Annual Financial Statement (Union Budget).  
	•	Article 123: Power of President to promulgate Ordinances during recess of Parliament. 
Union Judiciary & CAG (Articles 124–151)

	•	Article 124: Establishment and constitution of the Supreme Court of India. 
	•	Article 129: Supreme Court to be a Court of Record. 
	•	Article 131: Original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court. 
	•	Article 136: Special Leave to appeal by the Supreme Court (SLP). 
	•	Article 141: Law declared by the Supreme Court to be binding on all courts in India. 
	•	Article 143: Power of President to consult Supreme Court (Advisory Jurisdiction). 
	•	Article 148–151: Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG)—appointment, duties, and audit reports. 
Part VI: The State Governments (Articles 152–237)
The Executive & Legislature

	•	Article 153 & 154: Governors of States and executive power of the State. 
	•	Article 161: Power of Governor to grant pardons. 
	•	Article 163 & 164: Council of Ministers with Chief Minister at the head. 
	•	Article 165: Advocate-General for the State. 
	•	Article 169: Abolition or creation of Legislative Councils in States. 
	•	Article 200: Assent to Bills by Governor (including reserving bills for the President). 
	•	Article 213: Power of Governor to promulgate Ordinances. 
High Courts & Subordinate Courts

	•	Article 214 & 215: High Courts for States and High Courts to be Courts of Record. 
	•	Article 226: Power of High Courts to issue certain writs (broader scope than Art 32). 
	•	Article 233: Appointment of district judges.
