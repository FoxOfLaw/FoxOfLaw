# UNIT II — Constitutional Organs of Government

## A. PARLIAMENT

### 1. Composition of Parliament

Article 79: Parliament consists of the President, Rajya Sabha, and Lok Sabha.

#### Lok Sabha (House of the People)

- Members directly elected by citizens
- Five-year normal term; can be dissolved
- Money Bills originate only in Lok Sabha
- Council of Ministers collectively responsible to Lok Sabha

#### Rajya Sabha (Council of States)

- Permanent House; not subject to dissolution
- One-third members retire every two years
- Represents States and Union Territories
- Vice-President is ex officio Chairman

### 2. Parliamentary Sovereignty

- Parliament is subject to the Constitution, Fundamental Rights, judicial review, federal limitations, and basic structure doctrine. Constitutional supremacy > Parliamentary supremacy.

### 3. Parliamentary Privileges

- Article 105 (Parliament) and Article 194 (State Legislatures)
- Privileges: freedom of speech in Parliament, immunity for proceedings, regulate internal proceedings, punish contempt/breach of privilege
- Important cases: M.S.M. Sharma v Sri Krishna Sinha; Raja Ram Pal v Speaker, Lok Sabha (2007)

## B. EXECUTIVE

### President of India (Articles 52–62)

- Constitutional/formal head of the Union Executive
- Article 53 vests executive power in the President; Article 74 requires aid and advice of Council of Ministers

Powers:
- Executive (appointments: PM, ministers, governors, AG, CAG, Election Commissioners)
- Legislative (summon/prorogue Parliament, assent to Bills, ordinances Art.123)
- Financial (money bill recommendations, budget in President's name)
- Judicial (Article 72: pardon, reprieve, remission, commutation)

### Governor (Articles 153–162)

- Constitutional head of State; powers include executive, legislative, financial, judicial/pardoning; ordinance-making under Art.213
- Governor has limited discretion in certain situations

## C. JUDICIARY

- Integrated system: Supreme Court → High Courts → Subordinate Courts

### Supreme Court (Art.124)

Jurisdiction: original (Art.131); writ (Art.32); appellate (constitutional, civil, criminal); special leave (Art.136); advisory (Art.143); review (Art.137).

### High Courts (Art.214)

- Article 226: High Courts can issue writs for Fundamental Rights and other legal rights (broader than Art.32)

### Independence of Judiciary

- Security of tenure; difficult removal; salaries charged on Consolidated Fund; separation from executive; judicial review; contempt powers; institutional autonomy

### Appointment of Judges

- Collegium system evolved from the Three Judges Cases (S.P. Gupta; Second Judges Case; Third Judges Case)
- NJAC struck down in 2015 (Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v Union of India)
