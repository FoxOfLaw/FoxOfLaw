# UNIT IV — Emergency, Amendments and Freedom of Trade

## A. Freedom of Trade, Commerce and Intercourse (Articles 301–307)

- Article 301: trade, commerce and intercourse throughout India shall be free, subject to Arts.302–304
- Objective: maintain economic unity of India
- Restrictions: Parliament can impose restrictions in public interest (Art.302); States may impose taxes/restrictions subject to constitutional requirements (Art.304)
- Important cases: Atiabari Tea Co. v State of Assam; Automobile Transport (Rajasthan) Ltd. v State of Rajasthan; Jindal Stainless Ltd. v State of Haryana (2016)

## B. Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII)

Three types:
1. National Emergency — Article 352 (war, external aggression, armed rebellion; 44th Amendment replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion")
2. President's Rule — Article 356 (failure of constitutional machinery in a State)
3. Financial Emergency — Article 360 (threat to financial stability; never proclaimed)

### Effects of National Emergency

- Union executive power strengthened
- Parliament has expanded legislative authority over State subjects
- Article 358 and 359 affect Fundamental Rights (Arts.20 and 21 protected after 44th Amendment)

### President's Rule (Art.356)

- President may assume State functions; Parliament may legislate for the State
- S.R. Bommai v Union of India: Article 356 subject to judicial review; President's satisfaction not immune; federalism is important; majority should be tested on floor

## C. Amendment of Constitution (Article 368)

- Types: simple majority; special majority (majority of total membership + two-thirds of members present and voting); special majority + State ratification (half of State Legislatures)

### Doctrine of Basic Structure

- Kesavananda Bharati (1973): Parliament cannot destroy basic structure
- Elements: supremacy of Constitution, rule of law, judicial review, separation of powers, federalism, secularism, democracy, parliamentary system, free and fair elections, dignity and liberty, unity and integrity, limited amending power

Important amendment cases: Golaknath (1967), Kesavananda (1973), Indira Gandhi v Raj Narain (1975), Minerva Mills (1980), I.R. Coelho (2007)
