# UNIT III — Distribution of Powers between Union and States

## Legislative Relations (Articles 245–255)

Article 246 distributes legislative subjects between Parliament and State Legislatures via the Seventh Schedule (Union List, State List, Concurrent List).

- Union List: defence, foreign affairs, currency, railways, citizenship (Parliament)
- State List: police, public order, public health, agriculture, local government (States)
- Concurrent List: criminal law, marriage, education, forests, labour (both)

Residuary powers (Art.248): Parliament legislates on matters not enumerated; residuary powers to Union.

### Parliament's Power Over State Subjects

- Article 249: Rajya Sabha resolution for Parliament to legislate in national interest
- Article 250: during National Emergency
- Article 252: two or more States request Parliament to legislate for them
- Article 253: implement international treaties
- Article 356: President's Rule

## Administrative Relations (Articles 256–263)

- States must ensure compliance with parliamentary laws; Union can issue directions in permitted circumstances
- Article 263: Inter-State Council for coordination and dispute discussion

## Financial Relations (Articles 268–293)

- Distribution of taxes; grants-in-aid; Finance Commission (Art.280) recommends distribution of tax revenues and grants principles; GST framework; borrowing powers

## Important Constitutional Doctrines

1. Doctrine of Territorial Nexus — laws may have extra-territorial effect if sufficient territorial nexus (State of Bombay v R.M.D. Chamarbaugwala)
2. Doctrine of Pith and Substance — true nature/character of legislation determines validity (State of Bombay v F.N. Balsara)
3. Doctrine of Colourable Legislation — "what cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly" (K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo)
4. Doctrine of Repugnancy — Article 254; where State law conflicts with valid Parliamentary law, Parliamentary law prevails (M. Karunanidhi v Union of India)
5. Harmonious Construction — courts interpret provisions to allow both to operate where possible
