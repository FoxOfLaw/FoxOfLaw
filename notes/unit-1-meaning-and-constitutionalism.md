# UNIT I — Meaning and Concept of Constitution and Constitutionalism

## 1. Constitution — Meaning

A Constitution is the fundamental and supreme law of a country which establishes:

- Structure of government
- Powers and functions of governmental organs
- Relationship between different organs
- Distribution of powers between Union and States
- Rights and freedoms of individuals
- Limitations on governmental power

The Indian Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950.

### Functions of a Constitution

1. Establishes government — Creates Parliament, Executive and Judiciary.
2. Distributes powers — Especially between Union and State governments.
3. Limits governmental power — Fundamental Rights restrict arbitrary State action.
4. Protects individual liberty — Articles 14–32 provide constitutional protection.
5. Establishes rule of law — Government itself is subject to law.
6. Provides mechanisms for constitutional change — Article 368 provides amendment procedure.

## 2. Constitutionalism

Constitutionalism means governmental power must be limited and exercised according to constitutional rules.

### Constitution vs Constitutionalism

Constitution

- Fundamental legal document
- Creates government
- Contains powers
- Example: Indian Constitution

Constitutionalism

- Political/legal philosophy
- Limits government
- Controls exercise of powers
- Example: Rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review

### Elements of Constitutionalism

- Rule of law
- Limited government
- Separation of powers
- Judicial review
- Fundamental Rights
- Independent judiciary
- Democratic government
- Accountability
- Constitutional supremacy

## 3. Classification of Constitutions

### A. Written and Unwritten

- Written Constitution: rules in a written document (e.g., India, USA)
- Unwritten Constitution: rules from statutes, conventions, judicial decisions (e.g., UK)

### B. Rigid and Flexible

- Rigid: difficult amendment procedure (e.g., USA)
- Flexible: easily amended (e.g., UK)
- India is partly rigid and partly flexible

### C. Federal and Unitary

- Federal: powers divided between Union and States
- Unitary: central government predominant
- India: federal with strong unitary features

## 4. Sources and Framing of the Indian Constitution

Major sources and borrowed features:

- UK: Parliamentary government, Rule of Law, Cabinet system
- USA: Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Independence of Judiciary
- Ireland: Directive Principles
- Canada: Federation with strong Centre
- Australia: Concurrent List, Freedom of trade
- Germany: Emergency provisions
- USSR/Russia: Fundamental Duties
- South Africa: Amendment procedure
- Japan: Procedure established by law

Government of India Act, 1935 was a major source (federal structure, Governor, public service commissions, emergency provisions).

## 5. Constituent Assembly

- Chairman, Drafting Committee: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
- President of Constituent Assembly: Dr. Rajendra Prasad
- Objectives Resolution introduced by Jawaharlal Nehru
- Constitution adopted: 26 November 1949; came into force: 26 January 1950

## 6. Preamble

- Begins: "We, the People of India..." establishing popular sovereignty
- Describes India as: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic
- Objectives: Justice (social, economic, political), Liberty, Equality, Fraternity
- Kesavananda Bharati (1973): Preamble is part of the Constitution and subject to basic structure

## 7. Salient Features of Indian Constitution

- Written, detailed, sovereign Constitution; parliamentary government; federal structure with strong Centre; Fundamental Rights; Directive Principles; Fundamental Duties; independent judiciary; judicial review; universal adult franchise; single citizenship; secular state.

## 8. Is Indian Constitution Federal?

- Federal in structure but with strong unitary features
- Important case: S.R. Bommai v Union of India (1994)
